# New maps

Five new team maps for the round modes (Elimination, Tower, Bomb — and they join the Brawl
rotation too), and one big map built for Brawl (TDM / FFA) and Deathmatch practice, whose old
map (the Training Bay, 72 × 48 m) was far too cramped for ten players.

Every map has one idea that changes how you fight, not a gimmick you notice once: gravity you
can turn your back on, a sky that kills, light that gives you away, a floor that moves on a
schedule, a body you fight inside.

---

## Antipode — *the ship where the other team stands on your sky*

> Two crews wake in the same hollow hull and look up to find the enemy looking down.
> Between them hangs the Seam: a band of nothing where up forgets its name,
> and every bullet fired across it is a promise made to someone standing on the ceiling.

**Atmosphere.** A long cylindrical hull, hollowed out and gutted. Cold white service light on
the cyan deck below, amber emergency light on the orange deck above — each team's floor is the
other team's sky, windows and all. Loose cargo drifts through the middle, turning slowly.

**What makes it play differently.**
- **Two floors, two gravities.** Cyan's deck is the floor (gravity down). Orange's deck is the
  ceiling (gravity up). The map is the same after a half-turn: every spot on one deck has its
  twin on the other, upside down.
- **The Seam (zero-G).** A band through the middle of the hull where gravity switches off.
  Jump or jetpack into it and you drift. Push through the midline and the other deck's gravity
  takes you: you *fall up* onto the enemy's floor. Crossing is a commitment — you're in the open
  for a second, and both decks can see you.
- **The Spindle.** A thick column joins the two decks at the middle. Walk it with wall gravity:
  floor → column → ceiling. It's the slow, covered way across, and the fight over the column is
  the fight for the map.
- **Hull stairwells.** Along both long walls, corridors whose gravity bends: floor → wall →
  ceiling (like Kestrel's engine corridor). Flanks for patient players.
- Looking straight up to aim at people standing on your sky is disorienting at first and
  becomes the skill of the map.

**Sites.** Bomb site A on the floor deck in orange's half, site B on the ceiling deck in cyan's
half — each team defends a site on the *enemy's* deck.

## Stormglass — *an observatory with the floor ripped out*

> A weather station that the storm won.
> Brass and glass hang over a violet abyss where lightning crawls through the clouds,
> and the only road between the two fortresses is the air itself.

**Atmosphere.** Outdoors, high in a gas giant's upper atmosphere at violet dusk. Shattered
observatory domes, brass anemometer arms, glass floors you can see the drop through, lightning
flickering in the cloud sea far below. Wind-bent antenna masts.

**What makes it play differently.**
- **A 70 m open-air middle.** Between the two walled bastions there is no ground: just The Eye,
  a huge round void. Fall and you're gone. The middle is chaos by design — everyone sees
  everyone, and the fight is about height, angles and nerve.
- **Ways across, each with a cost.**
  - *The Glass Bridge* (north): straight and fast, and no cover at all.
  - *The Broken Span* (south): covered, but it snaps in the middle — a running jump across.
  - *The Anemometer* (centre): a floating ring platform with a mast, reached by launch pads
    from the bastion edges and zip-rails. The highest ground on the map and the most exposed.
  - Floating *shards*: small islands at different heights for hop routes.
- **Bastions are safe.** Each team's spawn fortress is walled, with covered exits — the chaos
  starts at the edge.

## Afterglow — *a night market that never closes*

> Rain on the skyglass, a city of lights turning below it.
> Noodle steam, a koi made of light circling an empty fountain,
> and every neon sign is a lamp that shows the enemy exactly where you are standing.

**Atmosphere.** The promenade deck of an orbital city at night, under a skyglass dome with a
planet's night side below — rivers of city lights. Pink, violet, teal and amber neon; paper
lanterns; stalls, awnings, vending machines; a monorail line running overhead.

**What makes it play differently.**
- **Light matters.** Ambient light is low and the signs are bright: alleys are dark, the plaza
  glows. Stand in front of a sign and you're a silhouette; lurk under an awning and you're a
  shadow.
- **Three heights.** Street level (stalls, alleys), rooftops (fire-escape stairs, awning hops),
  and the **monorail**: an elevated track straight across the map with a station in the middle
  — fast, high, and exposed — with zip-rails along it.
- **The Koi Plaza.** A sunken plaza in the middle around a dry fountain where a holographic koi
  circles: low cover, many entrances, the centre of everything.
- **Paper lantern screens** in the alleys: the Boomerang flies straight through them, nothing
  else does.

**Sites.** A: the arcade hall. B: the monorail station platform.

## The Orrery — *a clock the size of a cathedral*

> Someone built a model of their solar system out of brass, velvet and patience,
> then left it running in the dark.
> The planets still keep their appointments. Learn the schedule, and ride them.

**Atmosphere.** A dim observatory dome in deep space. At the centre, the Sun: a glowing brass
core on a pedestal. Around it, brass rings, spokes, gears the size of houses, star charts on the
walls, dark velvet red and old gold, warm lamps.

**What makes it play differently.**
- **The planets move.** Big planet platforms glide between the rings on fixed schedules
  (moving blocks): they wait, travel, wait, travel back. Ride one to cross the gap to the Sun
  or to a flank — or wait for it and get caught standing on a timer everyone can see.
  Everything is deterministic, so the timing can be learned.
- **Static routes too.** The outer ring walkway and the gear galleries underneath always
  connect everything (bots use these), so the planets are a shortcut, never a requirement.
- **The Sun.** The high ground in the middle, on the pedestal, reached by the planets or by
  the long spiral ramp.
- **Giant gears** as cover: tall full-cover teeth, half-cover hubs.

## Leviathan — *the fight inside a dead god*

> It died before the stars around it were born.
> Salvagers built their camps against its ribs and hung their lamps from its spine,
> and in its chest the heart still glows — not beating, not quite still.

**Atmosphere.** Inside the fossilised skeleton of a colossal space creature drifting through a
teal nebula. Ivory ribs arch overhead like a cathedral; bioluminescent teal and violet growths
light the bone; salvage scaffolds, cables and lamps cling to it. Stars and nebula show through
the gaps.

**What makes it play differently.**
- **The ribs.** A colonnade of tall rib arches down both sides: full cover that you can see
  *between* — long, broken sightlines that flicker as you move.
- **The Spine.** A walkway along the top of the backbone, reached by climbing vertebra steps —
  the high ground over the whole map, with no cover but the vertebrae.
- **The Heart.** A cavern in the middle around a crystallised heart hanging from the ribs,
  pulsing red-violet, with a ring balcony — the map's centre fight.
- **Head and tail.** The skull (north) is a bone cathedral with eye-socket windows — site A. The
  tail (south) coils into a spiral of vertebrae — site B.
- The teams' salvage camps are clamped to the two flanks.

## Colossus Yard — *a shipyard big enough for ten people to get lost in* (Brawl)

> They were building something the size of a mountain when the work stopped.
> The scaffolds still hold its bones up to the sunrise,
> and the yard around it is a maze of steel, cranes and long shadows.

**Atmosphere.** An orbital drydock at golden-hour sunrise over a planet. In the middle, the
half-built hull of a titan warship: open ribs, scaffolding, a spine deck high up, half-plated
interior decks. Around it: canyons of stacked containers, three drydock cranes with walkable
booms, a hangar full of parked fighters, fuel tanks, a freight line on the perimeter. Fall off
the platform's edge and it's a long way down to the planet.

**What makes it play differently.**
- **Size.** About 240 × 200 m — three times the old Deathmatch map — so ten players spread out
  and fights happen in pockets instead of one blender.
- **Every kind of fight in one place.** Container canyons (close range), the hull's interior
  decks (mid range), the crane booms and the spine deck (long range), the open apron (movement).
- **Launch pads** onto the crane booms, **zip-rails** along the freight line and between the
  cranes.
- **Many spawns, all sheltered.** 32 spawn points in alcoves around the yard, each with two or
  more ways out and none in view of a long lane.
